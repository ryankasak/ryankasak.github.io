<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Siouan Languages</h2>
<p class="research-desc">I examine various topics, ranging from word building to the interaction between prosody and information structure. Mandan is my primary focus, though I also look more broadly at the language family as a whole and work on fleshing out our understanding of Proto-Siouan. I suggest that the language isolate Yuchi may actually be a more divergent Siouan language.</p>

<h2 class="research-title">Caddoan Languages</h2>
<p class="research-desc">My research looks mostly at Caddo morphology. Specifically, Caddo verbal morphology and testing morpho-phonological processes based on internal word boundaries within the verb complex. Historical linguistic work on Northern Caddoan languages often omit cognates with Caddo, so reconstructing a fuller idea of what Proto-Caddoan looks like is still a work in progress.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">

<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Algonquian Languages</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Working on Southern New England Algonquian languages when I was living in Connecticut, I have been working through Pierson's Catechism to reconstruct a coprus of Quiripi and see how divergent it is from nearby language varieties. I am also involved in a project to examine voiceless vowels in Cheyenne and see how to best describe these sounds for both researchers and learners.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<img src="{{ '/images/research/historical.png' | relative_url }}" class="research-thumb" width="400" height="200" alt="Optimal tree with posterior propbabilities for Siouan languages including Yuchi">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Historical Linguistics</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Language always changes over time. A big part of my research program examines the word building properties of languages and looks to possible reasons for how those synchronic systems emerge. I am active in work towards a better understanding of Proto-Siouan and Proto-Caddoan, as well as efforts to see how Quiripi is distinct from neighboring Southern New England Algonquian. More recently, I am also investigating what sound changes have led to Cheyenne having such a high proliferation of voiceless vowels.</p>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="research-title">Language Documentation</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Language documenation and description plays a guiding role in my overall research program. I grew up having access to materials for my heritage language (i.e., Russian), and a primary goal of my work to increase the sum total of materials that others can use to access and understand their own heritage languages better. I welcome any discussions or requests to collaborate this process or requests.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<img src="{{ '/images/research/morphology.png' | relative_url }}" class="research-thumb" width="400" height="200" alt="Tableau of affix order in Mandan">

<h2 class="research-title">Morphology</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Word building is what I mainly prefer to research, though I enjoy looking at it through many different lenses. My dissertation looks at templatic morphology in Mandan and seeks to answer the question of why we cannot simply appeal to morphology itself to dictate how affixes are ordered, rather than conform to an extra-morpological system like syntax or phonology.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<img src="{{ '/images/research/phonology.png' | relative_url }}" class="research-thumb" width="400" height="200" alt="Spectrogram and waveform of a Cheyenne word">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Phonetics-Phonology Interface</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Differences in how speakers produce the sounds that make up words is not always guided by their underlying representations. I examine extra-phonological sound changes that result in elements to which the rest of the phonology is blind (e.g., Dorsey's Law vowels in Siouan), and I look at what articulatory phonetic conditions may result in de-voicing vowels in certain contexts (e.g., voiceless vowels in Cheyenne).</p>
</div>
